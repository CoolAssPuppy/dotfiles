# Competitive analysis

Conduct competitive analysis and create battle cards for technical products.

## Core principle

Competitive analysis is not about reacting. It is about proactively positioning your product to win in the minds of customers and decision makers. Honesty builds credibility, because customers will fact-check your claims.

## Voice and style

Apply the voice rules in the `writing` skill.

- Write at a 5th grade reading level. Short sentences. Easy to understand by everyone, including people new to the English language.
- Be honest about competitor strengths. Your credibility depends on it.
- Back every claim with evidence.
- Biased analysis misleads your own team before it misleads anyone else.

## Competitor identification

Look beyond the obvious. Map all four types of competition.

### Direct competitors

Companies offering similar products targeting the same audience.

How to analyze:
- Deep technical teardown
- Understand architecture choices
- Identify design philosophy
- Map feature parity

### Indirect competitors

Companies solving similar problems through different approaches.

How to analyze:
- Understand their mental model
- Map overlapping use cases
- Identify where they excel
- Find positioning gaps

Example: an opinionated team tool competing with a flexible database product.

### The status quo

The biggest competitor is often "do nothing." If it works and it is paid for, why change?

How to analyze:
- Understand switching costs
- Identify pain thresholds
- Find the moments where the pain becomes urgent
- Map organizational inertia
- Articulate the specific, measurable consequence of inaction for each stakeholder

To break through inertia, you must solve the top problem a stakeholder with spending authority cares about.

### Build-it-yourself teams

Some customers love building things. Your product may compete with internal solutions.

How to analyze:
- Understand build versus buy philosophy
- Calculate the true cost of building in-house
- Identify maintenance burden
- Find complexity thresholds

Question to ask: if your product can be quickly built in-house, should you rethink the product?

## Customer intelligence

The best competitive intelligence comes from customers.

### Win/loss interviews

Interview customers who:
- Chose you over competitors
- Chose competitors over you
- Evaluated both and chose neither

Questions to explore:
- How did you find each option?
- What were the evaluation criteria?
- What tipped the decision?
- What surprised you during evaluation?

### Discovery questions for sales

Add to sales calls:
- "What other solutions have you considered?"
- "How are you solving this problem today?"
- "What would make you switch?"
- "Who else is involved in this decision?"

### Customer surveys

Include competitive questions:
- "Did you consider alternatives before choosing us?"
- "What almost made you choose something else?"
- "How do you compare us to [competitor]?"

## Competitive positioning strategies

Choose your approach.

### Head-to-head

Directly compete on the same criteria.
- Best when you genuinely win on key dimensions
- Requires strong proof points
- Risk: legitimizes the competitor

### Reframe the criteria

Change what matters in the evaluation.
- Best when you are different, not just better
- Requires thought leadership to shift perspective
- Example: "Speed does not matter if you cannot scale"

### Niche down

Own a specific segment or use case.
- Best when you cannot win broadly
- Becomes the obvious choice for that niche
- Example: "The database for time-series data"

### Category creation

Define a new category you lead.
- Best when genuinely novel
- Requires significant investment
- Risk: market education burden

## Dynamic battle cards

Experiment with battle cards that update per customer. Combine:
- Static battle card content
- CRM data about the customer
- Intent signals
- Generated talking points personalized to the account

Output: customized talking points for each customer meeting.

### Continuous monitoring

- Set up alerts on competitor names
- Follow competitor social accounts

- Track press mentions

### Quarterly deep dives

- Full product re-evaluation
- Pricing and packaging check
- Content and SEO analysis
- Customer feedback synthesis

### Annual strategic review

- Market positioning assessment
- Changes across the competitive set
- New entrants evaluation
- Strategic recommendations

## Key principles

1. **Honesty builds credibility.** Customers will fact-check your claims. Being honest about competitor strengths makes your advantages more believable.
2. **Include your weaknesses.** Acknowledging where competitors are stronger builds trust and helps you win the right customers.
3. **Recommend competitors when appropriate.** This builds long-term trust and ensures you win deals where you can actually deliver value.
4. **Focus on use cases, not features.** Frame comparisons around what customers are trying to accomplish.

## What not to do

Voice and banned-language rules live in the `writing` skill. Skill-specific rules:

- Do not dismiss competitors as inferior across the board
- Do not use fear, uncertainty, and doubt as a tactic
- Do not make claims you cannot prove
- Do not ignore competitor strengths
- Do not pretend you win every deal. Understand why you lose.
- Do not ignore the status quo. Inaction is usually the biggest competitor.
- Do not obsess over features. Value matters more than feature checklists.
- Do not let battle cards go stale. Competitors evolve and your materials must too.
- Do not skip customer intelligence. The best insights come from real conversations.
- Do not write long documents. Make them scannable.
